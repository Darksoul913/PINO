Discretization Invariance Principle ($k \in [-16, 16]^2$):

Spectral convolution weights $R_\phi$ are parameterized on continuous low-frequency wave numbers k ∈ [-16, 16]². At $128\times 128$ or $256\times 256$ target meshes, the higher modes are zero-padded in Fourier space prior to the Inverse FFT ($\mathrm{irfft2}$), yielding exact continuous trigonometric polynomial evaluation rather than grid-bound pixel interpolation.

Governing Equations & Algorithmic Formulations

1. 2D Incompressible Navier-Stokes ($\omega$–$\psi$)
$$\frac{\partial \omega}{\partial t} + \mathbf{u} \cdot \nabla \omega = \nu \nabla^2 \omega + f(x, y)$$ $$-\nabla^2 \psi = \omega, \quad \mathbf{u} = \left(\frac{\partial \psi}{\partial y},\, -\frac{\partial \psi}{\partial x}\right)$$
2. Spectral Convolution Layer ($k_{\max} = 16$)
$$v_{l+1}(x) = \sigma\!\left(W_l v_l(x) + \mathcal{F}^{-1}\!\left(R_{\phi, l}(k) \cdot \mathcal{F}(v_l)(k)\right)(x)\right)$$ $$|k_x|, |k_y| \le k_{\max} = 16$$
3. Exact Fourier Derivatives & Biot-Savart
$$\widehat{\partial_x \omega}(k) = i k_x \hat{\omega}(k), \quad \widehat{\nabla^2 \omega}(k) = -|k|^2 \hat{\omega}(k)$$ $$\hat{\psi}(k) = \frac{\hat{\omega}(k)}{|k|^2}\;\;(k \neq 0), \quad \text{2/3 Orszag Dealiasing}$$
4. Multi-Objective PINO Training Loss
$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{PINO}} = \lambda_{\text{data}} \frac{\|\hat{u} - u\|_2}{\|u\|_2} + \lambda_{\text{ic}} \frac{\|\hat{u}_0 - a\|_2}{\|a\|_2} + \lambda_{\text{pde}} \|\mathcal{P}(\hat{u})\|_2$$
5. Test-Time Adaptation Loss (Projection-Only)
$$\begin{aligned} \mathcal{L}_{\text{TTA}}(\theta_{\text{proj}}) &= \|\mathcal{P}([\omega_0, u_\theta])\|_2^2 \\ &\quad + \alpha_{\text{anchor}} \|u_\theta - u_{\text{base}}\|_2^2 + \beta_{\text{circ}} (\bar{u}_\theta - \bar{\omega}_0)^2 \end{aligned}$$
6. Vectorized PSO Surrogate Control Update
$$v_i^{(t+1)} = w v_i^{(t)} + c_1 r_1 (p_i - x_i^{(t)}) + c_2 r_2 (g - x_i^{(t)})$$ $$x_i^{(t+1)} = \operatorname{clip}\!\left(x_i^{(t)} + v_i^{(t+1)},\, -\|f\|_\infty,\, \|f\|_\infty\right)$$

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

1. How do Validation MSE (~0.0049) and Relative L2 Error (~7.0–7.4%) relate mathematically?
The saved checkpoint validation MSE (0.0049) measures mean squared error, whereas Relative $L_2$ measures $\|\hat{u} - u_{\text{rk4}}\|_2 / \|u_{\text{rk4}}\|_2 = \sqrt{\text{MSE}} / \mathrm{RMS}(u_{\text{rk4}})$. With $\mathrm{RMS}(u_{\text{rk4}}) \approx 0.99$, $\sqrt{0.0049}/0.99 \approx 7.1\%$. By streaming 128,000 fresh trajectories across varied $(l, \alpha)$ and 3-step rollouts in train.py, training and held-out validation errors stay aligned (~7%) without overfitting.
2. How does zero-shot super-resolution work at $256^2$ with $k_{\max} = 16$ truncation and no retraining?
Each SpectralConv2d layer learns complex weights $R_\phi$ strictly on the continuous low-frequency wave numbers $k \in [-16, 16]^2$ (rather than pixel-grid filters). When evaluated with target_s=(256, 256), the output spectrum is zero-padded beyond mode 16 and inverted via irfft2 onto the denser mesh—evaluating the exact same continuous trigonometric polynomial at 16× higher spatial resolution.
3. Why does error increase on Out-Of-Distribution (OOD) flows, and how does TTA reduce it without labels?
Analytical flows like Vortex Dipoles and Kelvin-Helmholtz Shear layers lie outside the training Gaussian Random Field ($l=0.50, \alpha=2.50$) distribution. Test-Time Adaptation (TTA) freezes the 4 global Fourier layers and performs a few Adam steps on only the local ProjectionBlock ($64 \to 32 \to 1$) to minimize the exact spectral Navier-Stokes PDE residual over $[\omega_0, u_\theta]$, anchored by $\|u_\theta - u_{\text{base}}\|_2^2$ and mean circulation conservation. Calling restore_weights=True restores the checkpoint weights right after the demo.
4. Why is the ~1,000×+ speedup only seen under $256^2$ CFL scaling and not at $64^2$ single-step?
Classical explicit RK4 time-stepping is governed by the Courant–Friedrichs–Lewy (CFL) stability criterion $\Delta t \propto \Delta x$, forcing 4× more sequential time steps at $256^2$ than at $64^2$, with each step paying $O(N^2 \log N)$ FFT cost. By contrast, PINO maps $t=0 \to T$ in a single $O(1)$ forward pass. Therefore, while $64^2$ single-step speedups are modest (tens of $\times$), the speedup factor grows dramatically into the hundreds or ~1,000×+ regime at $256^2$ under CFL scaling.
5. How does PINO enable real-time Active Flow Control with Particle Swarm Optimization?
Optimizing a 4,096-dimensional forcing field $f(x, y)$ inside an iterative PSO swarm requires hundreds of PDE evaluations. Running a sequential RK4 solver inside every particle update would take tens of seconds or minutes. Wrapping PINO inside PSOSurrogateHarness batches all $N_{\text{particles}}$ perturbations simultaneously on GPU/MPS, enforcing the actuator bound $\|f\|_\infty \le B$ and minimizing terminal enstrophy $\frac{1}{2}\langle \omega^2 \rangle$ in well under a second.
